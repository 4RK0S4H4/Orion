1. Overall

The body is today's glass in all four. B to D add the lens rim (3px, medium strength, a little blur) in place of today's light line, with no glow, a faint glow (30% of today's) or today's glow. The nav bar keeps today's hairline here: docked bars are question 5.

2. Rim Width

The same on every side. Like today's edge it grows on standard-density screens (1.5x, 2x on large ones); these are the widths on a phone or retina screen. The body's corner shrinks by the same amount so the corners stay concentric.

3. Rim Strength

How much the rim deepens what is behind it: a little darker, then a color boost, then the hue turned back a few degrees (a boost alone pushes purple toward pink). The rim's own tint stays low (0 to 10%), so it reads as clear colored glass rather than a line. On a light page the slight darkening also gives the edge a faint gray line over white.

4. Rim Blur

With no blur the rim shows the content behind it sharp, like the edge of a lens; with the body's blur it is a frosted band that is only brighter and more colorful.

5. Docked Bars

A docked bar (the nav bar here, also the scrolled top bar and the site header) draws one hairline today on the edge that faces the content (decision 0064). B gives it the lens rim along that one edge instead, the bar's body unchanged. The floating glass above it has the lens rim in both.

Where Glass Turns Solid

High contrast, reduced transparency, an element marked data-glass="off" and browsers without backdrop filters keep today's solid surfaces: the lens is built inside the same conditions, so it simply is not drawn. This is how those surfaces look (no rim, no glow, no see-through).
