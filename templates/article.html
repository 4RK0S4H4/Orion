Six Minutes in Luxor

The longest totality on land this century crosses the Nile at lunchtime. Here is where to stand, what the sky will do, and how to keep the day from going wrong.

ANAmara NwosuEleven totalities, two of them clouded out · 9 min read · Updated 12 Nov 2026

On 2 August 2027 the Moon’s shadow comes ashore at Cádiz, crosses the Strait of Gibraltar, runs the length of North Africa and leaves over the Red Sea. Anywhere under it, the day goes dark for minutes. Under the middle of it, in Luxor, it stays dark for six minutes and twenty seconds, longer than anyone standing on land will see again until 2114.

That length is the reason to go. The first two minutes of any totality go by in a blur of shouting. The last four are the ones you remember.

Why Luxor, and Not the Coast

The shadow is fastest where it first touches the Earth and slowest near the point of greatest eclipse, which falls in the desert just outside Luxor. Every city on the path gets its own length of darkness:

  1. Cádiz4 m 30 s
  2. Tangier4 m 45 s
  3. Benghazi5 m 50 s
  4. Luxor6 m 20 s
  5. Jeddah5 m 40 s

The coast has its own pull: Cádiz is a short flight for most of Europe and the Sun stands high over the sea. But Atlantic mornings in August can bring a bank of sea fog, and in Luxor the chance of a cloud in early August is close to none.

The Day, Minute by Minute

An eclipse is a slow hour, a fast six minutes and a slow hour again. Pick where you will stand to see your times.

Your Eclipse in Luxor

Local time, Egypt (UTC+3)

    Wear eclipse glasses for every minute of the partial phases. Take them off only when the last bead of light is gone, and put them back on the moment it returns.

    What to Pack

    • Two pairs of eclipse glasses marked ISO 12312-2. Someone always sits on the first pair.
    • A white cloth or a straw hat. Through the gaps in a hat brim the ground fills with little crescent Suns.
    • Water, and shade until the last minute. Luxor in August passes 40 °C by noon; totality cools it by about five degrees.
    • A phone on a tripod, set to record and left alone. Look up, not at the screen.
    “I photographed my first totality and remember nothing of it. I watched the second one and can still tell you the colour of the horizon.”

    If It Clouds Over

    In Luxor it almost certainly won’t. On the coast, check the forecast the evening before and be ready to drive inland at dawn: sea fog rarely reaches more than 20 km from the shore, and the shadow is 250 km wide.
